
\chapwithtoc{Conclusion}

In this thesis, we studied the use of neural networks for classification of $3$ magnetic phases, in spin configurations obtained via Monte Carlo simulation. We started by establishing the settings, that enable the machine learning model to reliably recongnize the $3$ phases: ferromagnet, skyrmion and magnetic spiral, on the clean, single phase configurations.  Our main goal was to identify regularization and modeling strategies that improve the model's behavior in regions without clear ground-truth labels, where phases mix smoothly.

We proposed and used a custom metric to evaluate model consistency on phase transitions without relying on explicit labels. This allowed us to systematically compare model architectures and training strategies in a way that focuses on physically meaningful behavior.

Our experiments showed that simple convolutional networks consistently outperformed dense networks and even more sophisticated CNN variants. In particular, full convolutions proved better than depthwise separable convolutions, and architectural add-ons like Squeeze-and-Excite or CBAM did not bring improvements for this task. We also observed that deeper networks tend to perform worse on phase transitions despite excellent standard test-set performance, highlighting the importance of targeted evaluation metrics.

Among optimizers, RMSprop combined with a plateau-based learning rate schedule delivered the best results, significantly improving both convergence speed and stability. Using global average pooling instead of flattening, singnificantly reduced the number of parameters, while maintaining the same performance on the test set and further improving the performance on the skyrmion-spiral transition.

Regarding data augmentation, CutMix achieved the most substantial improvement on the skyrmion–spiral phase transition, improving the out-of-order metric by about $30\%$ compared to the best non-augmented baseline. However, it also degraded performance on the ferromagnet–skyrmion transition, where even unaugmented models achieved perfect results, as measured by our metric. In contrast, MixUp did not significantly affect the skyrmion–spiral transition but slightly improved behavior on the ferromagnetic–skyrmion boundary. The overall the best results were obtained by a model trained on the CutMixes of skyrmion and spiral lattices, together with MixUps of ferromagnets and skyrmion lattices.

We also investigated dropout as a regularization technique. Contrary to common expectations, dropout neither improved test performance nor helped with phase transitions. In fact, it tended to negate the positive effects of augmentation. Using Monte Carlo dropout at inference did not noticeably improve model behavior.

Overall, we have explored a wide range of machine learning methods and systematically mapped which approaches work well and which do not for this classification task. Most importantly, we have shown the impact of using non-physical data augmentations on model performance in during the phase transitions.
